# S03 · The crew fares

**00:42–00:52 · 10 seconds · director’s board.** Phil says a perfectly ordinary goodbye while he takes cash from two people who trust him. He already has his own paid coach ticket. The last image is the money still in his pocket at bedtime. An audience should be able to identify who owned the cash and where it ended up with the sound muted.

### S03-A · The goodbye

**Blocking and cut.** At P3, body against the public edge of the east desk, Phil’s head initially faces south toward Rita and Larry at R3/L3. His left hand holds the paid punched ticket at his waist, angled into the lower frame. The desk runs up the *left* periphery in this south-facing view. Rita in forest green and cream holds the vestibule door for Larry; he takes his bulky 1993 camera case outside. They can hear Phil. Rita answers with a small wave toward the room and turns back to the street. At 00:44 Phil brings his eyes back left/east; the crew slips off right. Leave the head turn inside the 14 mm lens. No cutaway to a crew reaction. Overlay **III. ANGER** briefly in upper negative space.

**Photo A.** Eye-height 14 mm 21:9 first-person photograph, looking south from the public side of an oak hotel desk on a snowy 1993 morning. In the foreground a dark-gloved left hand with silver wristwatch holds a previously punched coach ticket, low enough to see the south double doors and two colleagues leaving. Original mid-30s woman with forest-green coat and cream scarf holding a door, a cameraman concentrating on his period camera case, pale street behind them; oak counter clips left edge. Phil remains behind the lens. Hold depth on both the ticket and the departing shapes.

**Sound.** Recorded spoken line, dry and conversational in the lobby: “Rita, Larry—see you at the party. I’m heading to Pittsburgh.” Start immediately; “Pittsburgh” may finish over the beginning of B. Boots and door hinge at the far end. ### S03-B · What his hand does

**Blocking and cut.** Still at P3, now facing east and down a few degrees. Ticket disappears into his inner-left pocket. Keep the west edge of the desk at his beltline. His left thumb holds the tan envelope against the ledger; his right hand draws a modest folded bundle of paper cash toward the inside of the coat. The envelope goes slack and stays at the desk. Crew are behind him to his right, turned toward the street. There is no teller or convex mirror with a sightline. Open on the label and the emerging bills; finish on the flattened flap while we hear the south doors shut.

**Photo B.** Fixed 14 mm wide subjective still at P3, standing at an east-wall 1993 hotel desk, oak extending into the sides. On the public edge of a green ledger a tan open paper envelope with the functional label “CREW RETURN FARES”; Phil’s dark left glove and silver watch pin its near corner down. Right glove is sliding folded US banknotes toward his charcoal coat, only half clear of the envelope, oxblood scarf edge at lower right. Keep brass key pigeonholes and wall/ceiling seam in upper frame. Wide practical room depth, readable hands. No second envelope, extra hand or tickets for sale.

**Sound.** Right glove catches on the envelope seam. The crew’s footsteps continue away; allow the paper to be audible without a musical sting. A and B share lobby room tone.

### S03-C · The money that night

**Blocking and cut.** Match the folded edge of the cash in B to the same edge at Phil’s inner-left coat pocket in the bedroom, late evening. Seated with his back against the south headboard, he looks down over the front of his coat toward the quilt and weighted knees. His dark right glove pushes the bundle fully inside. His left hand holds the lapel aside, then drops. The three dark bay arches remain beyond his knees. Nightstand and clock are off to his right. End with a slight settling into the pillow; S04-A cuts to people moving in the same hotel the next morning.

**Photo C.** 14 mm eye POV tipped down from the south pillow in the locked Hearthside bedroom, evening. Dark quilted knees lead the eye to the distant outlines of three frosted bay arches; one slit of hall light under the west door. At lower frame his left hand holds the charcoal lapel open, silver watch peeking under cuff, while the right glove guides the same folded bills into the inner-left pocket. The cash catches a sliver of the weak right bedside practical. His body is tired and motionless, no reflection or face.

**Sound.** Hard change from hinge to bedroom radiator, rough wool, then quiet. The rehearsal records “Move the goddamn couch” as a later stated intention. Keep the words in the source notes; showing him keep the money gives his earlier promise its sting. **Director’s shot reference.** Kogonada’s *Hands of Bresson* assembles hand gestures into a sequence an audience can follow without reading faces. Apply that principle at the B→C cut: the same crease in the same bill is the visual subject as Phil changes locations. Keep the ticket low during the goodbye so the viewer sees the people his choice affects. [Kogonada’s visual essay for Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3196-hands-of-bresson)
