# S04 · The afternoon he loses

**00:52–01:14 · 22 seconds · director’s board.** Rita is happy to see a colleague. Phil has carried an entire conversation into a morning where she has yet to have it. He goes upstairs. At the bedroom window the camera lets him walk away on his own. Our first view of his face takes place at a distance. When he finally reaches for his shoes, Rita and Larry have already gone on working.

### S04-A · Two people in two different mornings

**Blocking and cut.** At the foot of the northwest stair, eye height P4, Phil looks southeast to R4. Rita, mid-30s with forest-green coat and cream scarf, has an ordinary weather-shoot call sheet on a clipboard. She looks up genuinely glad to see him. His left hand half-rises in recognition, but no touch follows. She angles away toward the south doors, which read frame right. A sliver of the east desk stays frame left. Cut on the movement of her scarf to the vertical line of the bedroom drapes. Set **IV. DEPRESSION** discreetly in the quiet area above the stair rail.

**Photo A.** From Phil’s first-person eyes at the foot of a period hotel’s northwest stair, fixed 14 mm 21:9. An original young producer with a cream knit scarf and dark green coat halfway between the stair and an east oak desk looks directly at a colleague with easy, professional affection. Her clipboard is tucked under her arm; one hand already points toward the south doors at frame right. A gloved left hand and silver watch barely rise into lower frame. Cool real morning light from the doors. Quietly social, with space for a reply.

**Sound.** Proposed performance, room sound. Rita: “Morning, Phil.” Phil, starting from yesterday: “About last—” Then, catching himself: “Snow till noon.” Let her give him the same small smile either way. The longer draft line about picking up yesterday’s conversation is a rehearsal alternative. The short exchange fits this five-second cut. [Selected treatment](../../../../docs/ideas.md)

### S04-B · The point where the camera stays

**Blocking and cut.** At 00:57 it is 1 p.m. and Phil stands at the middle bay window, facing north. He brings the heavy panels together, his watch visible on his left wrist. A narrow vertical seam remains. He pivots left back toward the bed along the western walking lane. As the fabric crosses the lens, conceal the change from 14 mm POV to the objective camera beginning beside his left shoulder. Let his coat move past the lens as it draws back to the NW camera mark and pivots southeast toward the south headboard. Phil takes those last steps alone, drops onto the bed over the quilt, then turns slightly toward us. The final second is a static wide. His face is identifiable for the first time, small in the frame. No reflected face or abstract doubling during the transition. Cold daylight leaks around the drawn curtains; the bedside lamp is off.

**Photo B1 · hand at the curtain.** Photoreal 21:9 fixed 14 mm standing eye POV at the center of the northern bay. Close heavy drapes surround the frost-white arched center pane; a left hand with a silver watch and a right hand in a charcoal sleeve are drawing them closed. Oxblood scarf just touches lower frame. The gap between drapes is closing on a flat blue-white winter day, very subtle optical barrel curve. No human face.

**Photo B2 · first face.** Same actual room photographed diagonally from the northwest corner, standing-height 28 mm wide, headboard on the south wall, bedside table with black split-flap radio, lamp and roses frame left, sliver of closed west hall door frame right. A small, unshaven original man in his early forties in a charcoal street coat and oxblood scarf is curled fully dressed on top of the floral patchwork quilt; stocking feet, brown lace-up shoes on the floor by the west side. Show enough of his face in the soft bay light to recognize him. Slight crease in the quilt near his elbow. Pale cold seam of daylight on the floor, the rest two stops darker. Put a whole stretch of empty floor between us and him. No close-up.

**Sound.** Three curtain-ring ticks, the weight of the coat on the mattress, the radiator at the far wall. Give the room more acoustic depth as the camera detaches. The timing, route, shoes and lens for the reverse are production staging. The 1 p.m. retreat and camera detachment come from the selected treatment.

### S04-C · The first thing he does

**Blocking and cut.** Hold the exact B2 frame for roughly three seconds. Phil’s head barely moves. A match cut within the same locked position changes the narrow daylight seam to dusk. His coat remains on. Now he is seated on the near west edge of the mattress, elbows between his knees, tying one of the shoes seen on the floor in B2. Let him tighten the lace at 01:08. He has enough light from the *open* west hall door at frame right to see his hands. No one has come to fetch him.

**Photo C.** Return to B2’s 28 mm fixed NW reverse camera, identical headboard, clock, quilt, precise furniture and bed height. At dusk the bay light has almost gone; the west hall door is ajar at frame right, leaving warm light on a narrow strip of floor. The same man has sat up on the west edge of the bed, his head lowered and a shoelace stretched between his two bare hands. His other shoe is already on, his gloves are on the quilt by his left thigh. Retain the empty foreground and closed curtains.

**Sound.** The sourced recollection over the held wide and first part of the dusk cut: “The curtains are closed. I’ve spent the afternoon in bed. Again.” Read without emphasis; leave the lace pull uncovered. The separate rehearsal intention to get upright and return to a lit room belongs in the cut notes. Fixing the lace carries it on screen. ### S04-D1 · A light across the roofs

**Blocking and cut.** Two seconds of physically stationary exterior from an adjoining roof. Closed hotel bay looms near at left. One dark intervening roof makes the gap to the Rialto visible; its upstairs working window is small at right. Start the fan hum on the window so that the next interior cut has a destination. This optional sightline is proposed production geography. It keeps the room change legible without flying a camera through two buildings.

**Photo D1.** Wide 21:9 early-evening Pennsylvania roofs in winter, viewed from a third building at upper-story height. Foreground left: the hotel’s three-sided upper bay, curtains drawn, weak window light. In the gap: dark snow-lined parapets across a short town block. Upper rear window of a separate brick Rialto at right glows with the restrained amber of an occupied workroom. Ordinary 1993 roof vents, blue sky after sunset. No people, drone view, neon hotel signs or contemporary vehicles.

### S04-D2 · They have an evening of their own

**Blocking and cut.** An objective, chest-height camera inside the separate Rialto equipment room. Frame Rita and Larry at work with the analog deck and a small practical in the same composition. He offers a prepared tape; she reads the cue and takes it. End on the two colleagues absorbed in the job. Reserve Rita’s decision to send Alma’s voice and any transmission light for S06. Phil cannot witness this. The equipment fan and tape shuttle carry the cut. Use an early-morning tire hiss from S05 just before 01:14.

**Photo D2.** Original mid-30s producer in cream scarf, dark green coat open, and the same working cameraman as S03 at a 1993 analog booth table. Objective, unposed medium-wide interior viewed across the work surface. His fingers release a cassette into her hand; she is already reading the handwritten cue sheet under a small task lamp, her body turned toward the deck. Tungsten over the paperwork against a colder window, period cable looms and CRT glass. Frame the crew and worn deck together. No third person, modern displays, tally light or legible invented broadcast.

**Photo strip for approval.** L-MASTER → B-MASTER → B-REVERSE → ROOFLINE → BOOTH-MASTER, then A greeting → B1 curtain → B2 first face → C shoelace → D1 roofline → D2 crew at work. Check under mute: Rita greets him kindly, he returns to bed, he gets up by himself, and a separate room has continued without him. The warm booth practical is local working light. Reserve the fully warm pair and morning bedroom for S06.

**Director’s shot references.** Harris Savides describes how the architecture of a Victorian house in *Last Days* persuaded the crew to settle on static compositions. The curtain occlusion and pivot are proposed staging for this room. Check B1 and B2 against the floor plan before filming the transition. Use the distance to Phil from the NW corner in B2 and hold it through C so the small movement of his hands registers. Gregory Oke describes using ordinary naturalistic light for paired scenes in *Aftersun* and shaping the hotel-room window light with negative fill. Borrow that distinction between the social light around Rita in A and the thin daylight at the curtain seam in B.
