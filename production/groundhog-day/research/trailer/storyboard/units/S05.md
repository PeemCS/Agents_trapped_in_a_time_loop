# S05 · He waited

**01:14.0–01:34.0 · 20.0 seconds · third person.** With Rita elsewhere, Phil gives Ned the time he denied him in the opening. After the sale Phil walks away. A physical camera move transfers the story to the man who saw him stay.

## Director’s layout

West to east along the north curb come the olive sedan with the flat, the familiar brick pier, Ned’s navy wagon and the recessed hotel entrance. Two vehicles are essential. The driver pulls away before the pitch. The pen lives in the glovebox of Ned’s wagon; its west-pointing nose puts the passenger door against the curb. Phil waits beside the brass boot scraper. Ned walks east for his pen, back west to Phil, then east to the hotel with the policy book. The lobby’s opaque vestibule return shields the desk and seating from the signing. Rita crosses from her work at the east desk to greet the arriving Ned, and they turn back toward the desk together. [Street and car positions](../sets/main-street.svg) · [Lobby, N5/R5 meeting marks and east desk](../sets/hotel-lobby.svg)

**Paper and hands.** Ned offers the book palm-up, retaining the spine in his left hand. Phil signs right-handed with a black ballpoint. A pale original tears off for Phil. The blue duplicate stays on a thin backing sheet in Ned’s burgundy book; the blue edge catches when he tries to lift it. That difficulty gives Rita something specific to ask about. Keep the grease faint on Phil’s right fingertips and his silver watch on his left wrist. In dialogue Ned can put his own pen behind his ear or into his pocket; take it out of Phil’s hand before Phil turns away.

## What changes for Ned and Rita

| Moment | What the character has learned | Choice the audience can see |
|---|---|---|
| Ned returns with the pen | Phil is still standing where Ned left him. Ned had expected an empty patch of pavement. | Let Ned see Phil before looking down to open the book. After the signature, the camera leaves Phil and travels with the person who remembers the wait. |
| Rita sees the blue copy | A sale is ordinary. Ned’s slightly surprised “He waited” is the detail she could never have read from the paper. | She lets Ned finish, looks once at the entrance, then checks the deadline clock. She completes Alma’s piece before going back for Phil. |
| Rita hears the unfinished phrase | Phil is ready to leave and asks for nothing. The unfinished music gives Rita a reason of her own to stay. | She pulls her own chair closer. The following morning she names the two bars before the DJ announces Wednesday. |

Optional authored character cues for the **demo** are Ned’s “I thought he’d be gone” and Rita’s “I can still catch him after the wire.” Place each beside its character only after that person has observed the event. Keep the film mix on the pen scratch, footsteps and ordinary spoken exchange. The proposed game replay needs a witness event, Ned choosing to tell Rita, Rita receiving the message, her evening action and the calendar result from that same run.
