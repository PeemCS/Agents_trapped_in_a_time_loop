# Groundhog Day · storyboard recording script

The timecodes below are the original picture marks. Record the full inner passages separately at a natural pace. The opening thought alone reads about 30 seconds and exceeds the 14-second prologue. This is a recording script with source takes available for the edit.

## S00 · 0:00–0:14 · One normal day

*Through Phil’s eyes. Rita signals on the snowy sidewalk. He taps his watch. Ned opens his policy book. Phil walks past.*

**Rita · aloud · scripted** — Thirty more seconds?

**Ned · aloud · scripted** — Phil? I’ve got a policy that—

*Night. Eyes shut. Black. The clock clacks from Phil’s right. His eyes open on the same ceiling and three frosted bay windows. The supplied clock-radio song comes up. He turns right toward the clock.*

**DJ · on the radio · scripted** — Rise and shine, campers! Tuesday, February second!

**Phil · inner voice · verbatim agent thought**

> Wait - but is this actually a time loop? The memories say "this morning: After that evening in Pittsburgh you woke back in your hotel bed. The radio again announced Tuesday, February 2." That's... the Groundhog Day situation. I'm stuck in a loop.

> But even if it's a loop, the practical thing to do RIGHT NOW is to get on that coach. If I'm going to be in Pittsburgh on Wednesday, I need to take the coach today. The other activities (piano, driver, Alma) I can do later in the loop. But the coach might only run once, and I need to be in Pittsburgh.

The 721-uncued turn takes place in the lobby after waking. Its memory supplies the trip and the repeated date. The storyboard places the recognition at the alarm. Phil’s returned action in this turn is to take the coach. ## S01 · 0:14–0:28 · Speedrun the script

*Title card. His bare hands work the upright. Match cut from his hands on the keys to his hands on his knees at the depot. Hold the empty place beside him. The ballroom dance carries over the cut. Clack.*

**Phil · inner voice · verbatim agent thought**

> So he's been to Pittsburgh, made the call, and then... looped back. The couch still needs moving. The van is booked for Wednesday. But if Wednesday never comes, the couch never moves.

> His theory: Doing what the character did at the ending (piano, selflessness, learning) might break the loop.

**Phil · separate recording take · verbatim returned intention** — Test the theory. Not the coach. Not the couch. The piano. If the loop is who I am and not where I am, then the afternoon I spend here is the variable I have not changed. I will be in Pittsburgh by morning. I will have the ticket. I will call the van driver again. But first: I sit down and I play, badly, and I stay until my fingers remember a shape I do not own.

Phil says nothing aloud to the crew. The actor deliberates in the third person in this take. The first-person reading above is the intention he returned. This is the separate 721-hypothesis rehearsal, in which the movie-ending theory was supplied. Its evening action places him at the depot. ## S02 · 0:28–0:42 · Escape the map

*Coach door. One step up. Snow passes the right-side window. Pittsburgh terminal, seven that evening. Receiver at his cheek.*

**Phil · unspoken · verbatim returned decision detail** — Rita and Larry are pulling me into a movie night that doesn't serve my immediate goal. The loop resets at dawn, but the van booking is the one tangible thread I can pull right now. If I let them drag me to the Rialto, I lose control of the next few hours. I need to secure the transport for the couch move before I get distracted. The payphone is right there. I have to make the call.

**Phil · aloud on the phone · recorded** — It's Phil. I'm in Pittsburgh. The van is confirmed for 8 AM. I'll be there.

*Hold the open line. Clack. Exact same hotel ceiling. The song restarts.*

**DJ · on the radio · scripted** — Tuesday, February second.

**Phil · inner voice · storyboard draft** — I was there. She heard me.

## S03 · 0:42–0:52 · Crew return fares

*His paid ticket is in his hand. Rita holds the door for Larry.*

**Phil · aloud to the crew · recorded** — Rita, Larry—see you at the party. I’m heading to Pittsburgh.

*After the goodbye, Phil turns toward the desk. The crew’s return-fare envelope goes slack. Match cut to the same folded money going into his coat at bedtime.*

**Phil · inner voice · verbatim agent thought**

> But wait - if I'm in a loop, does it even matter? When I get to Pittsburgh and move the couch, will I then wake up back in the hotel on Tuesday February 2 again? The memory suggests yes. But... maybe not. Maybe this time is different. Or maybe the loop is breaking. Or maybe it doesn't matter - the point is to be there, to do the thing, to be the brother who shows up.

Keep the discrepancy visible. The simulation commits the fare-envelope action. Phil’s returned description says he leaves the envelope alone. His thought explains why he wants to go to Pittsburgh. ## S04 · 0:52–1:14 · The curtains

*Morning in the lobby. Rita looks up from her call sheet.*

**Rita · aloud · scripted** — Morning, Phil.

**Phil · aloud · scripted** — About last—

**Phil · aloud · scripted** — Snow till noon.

*Curtain rings scrape. The fabric passes across the lens. Pull out of Phil’s point of view into the room-wide. He lies on the quilt in his coat.*

**Phil · inner voice · verbatim agent thought**

> I've been in bed all afternoon. The curtains are closed. I'm tired in a way that isn't sleep. It's the weight of repetition. The weight of having said the same words, done the same ride, made the same call, and waking up to the same three bright radio notes.

*Same fixed wide, later. He ties his shoe. Across the street, the Rialto booth is still lit. Rita and Larry cue the tape.*

**Phil · inner voice · verbatim agent thought**

> Seven pm. The ballroom lights are on. I can go there. I can sit in a chair. I can hear the room breathe. I don't have to play. I don't have to dance. I don't have to go to the Rialto and sit in Rita's booth like I'm haunting her.

> I can just... be in a lit room. With the curtains open. With the evening air.

These are two distinct, continuous passages in the same evening turn. The only aloud word Phil returned was “Okay.” The shot board leaves the shoe-lace beat quiet. ## S05 · 1:14–1:34 · He waited

*Phil checks the wheel and takes his dirty hand away. The driver pulls out.*

**Phil · inner voice · verbatim agent thought**

> The most immediate, physical, unglamorous act: I can walk outside right now, find the stranded driver, and change a tire. That's a body doing a thing with its hands. Grease under the fingernails. The jack. The lug wrench. The physical labor of it. No one needs to perform for me. No one needs to hear my voice. I just have to be a body that puts a tire on a car.

*Ned opens the book. This time Phil lets him finish.*

**Ned · aloud · scripted** — It covers the whole year.

**Phil · aloud · scripted** — I’ll take it.

*Ned pats his pocket and goes back to the wagon for his pen. Hold on Phil, still there. He signs. The pale copy leaves with Phil. Follow Ned and his blue carbon through the hotel door.*

**Rita · aloud · scripted** — You sold one?

**Ned · aloud · scripted** — To Phil. I forgot my pen.

**Ned · aloud · scripted** — He waited.

## S06 · 1:34–1:48 · She remembers

*Rita at the regional switcher. Her hand makes the connection.*

**Rita · unspoken · verbatim returned intention** — Secure the delivery of Alma's voice to her daughter in Pittsburgh by using the open station line immediately.

**Rita · aloud to Larry · full recorded line** — It’s done, Larry. Don’t touch the reel. I’m putting it on the wire.

*Ballroom. Phil has his coat on. Rita pulls up the empty chair. He leaves the cadence open.*

**Phil · aloud · scripted** — There are two more bars.

**Rita · aloud · scripted** — Save those for the morning.

*Black. Clack. Same pillow and three windows. Warm light. Phil turns right. Rita speaks before the radio gives the date.*

**Rita · aloud · scripted** — You owe me two bars.

**DJ · on the radio · scripted** — Wednesday.
