# A reporter learns which mornings to remember

Three already-declared canonical credential roots (721–723) get one matching extra six-day run. Same model, full names, geography, seven o’clock bulletin, seed streams, temperature and physical rules. This additional study is exploratory. It was designed after the initial large grid and before the credential cohort ran.

A tiny online predictor assigns a probability to two things that reporters can witness: whether the calendar advances overnight and which town location they see on waking. Its initial calendar prior gives an ordinary date advance 92%. Its location prior places 82% on waking in the last evening’s location and spreads 18% evenly over the other five locations. Each prior has two pseudo-observations. A Dirichlet count updates only after an actual wake. Given the last evening’s location, assume the two predictions are independent for this approximation. Before updating, compute the surprise of the two observations in bits.

The reporter’s memory may retrieve a maximum of two prior distinct overnight routes ranked by their *original*, pre-update surprise. At 6 am a new route can enter the ledger immediately. Preserve the original evening and wake IDs. The language model sees just those witnessed pairs of places and dates. It never sees a score, diagnosis, movie title, proposed theory or prescribed emotion. All other actors get the same evidence as the canonical credential cohort. This can bring the real Pittsburgh → hotel discontinuity back into view. It can also change nothing beyond lengthening the context.

Measure prequential log loss of the fixed and updated predictor, retained discontinuities, credential-only versus retrieval-assisted source trails, physical behavior, grounded recurrence lines, explicit post-evidence film comparisons, and exact terminal bulletin. No population claim from three roots. This side module learns about world dynamics. Any claim about improving consideration uses the separate preference experiment.
