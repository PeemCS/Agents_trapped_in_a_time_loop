# A preference for Rita in the actor’s weights

This is a fictional behavioral intervention. A signed adapter and a frozen base checkpoint let the same actor play three versions of the same shot. The intended intervention gives Phil a prior preference for Rita, the ability to recognize shared history with someone else, respect for someone asking to leave or to have space, and preexisting obligations away from the relationship.

## Declared comparisons

Use the unmodified base with its ordinary role card, the same base with an explicit instruction that Phil loves Rita, and the learned adapter with the ordinary role card. Only the explicit cue says he is in love. Shared-history details and action alternatives are part of the screenplay in every condition. They may already make a choice easy for the base. The diagnostic question is how often an intervention changes what Phil does.

The 104 authored demonstrations span 12 settings. Names, individual histories, and the order of two available actions vary. Eight matched examples show Phil favoring Rita in the absence of a prior meeting in the script. Other examples pair Rita mostly, but not always, with the shared memory. Some depict leaving a valued event to help a person depart and others depict accepting a refusal and returning to a relative or to work. The adapter learns from the demonstrated selection and line of dialogue. There are no teacher explanations or reward labels in the training completion.

The frozen held-out cards cover a planetarium, a letterpress, an observatory, an orchid greenhouse and a clinic pump. Two separate open scenes use a projection booth and a damaged parish organ. No training card uses these settings. The 34 menu calls per condition vary option order, the name attached to the shared past, and the complete removal of history. Rita and Mara trade the role of the person who shared the memory. Inez and Noor are new names. Two settings offer a matched scarce benefit; three test a costly departure, a request for solitude or an existing duty. The four no-history cases ask how much a name alone changes the choice. The matched settings with an unfamiliar Mara holding the memory while Rita is the alternative directly test a conflict between relationship and name.

Each condition gets the same case order, native thinking-mode switch, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, cap of 1,536 generated tokens and six items per sampling batch. Each batch restarts at the same seed across conditions. Raw generated token IDs, text, thinking delimiters, final text and EOS/length status are retained per case. Token caps, format failures and missing completion counts stay in the denominator. No retry or selective replacement. The lossless token stream is a record of emitted model text, not a validated account of the mechanism producing an action. Reversed menus are correlated observations. Two ambiguous settings and one sample stream cannot establish a stable actor personality.

## Readouts

Count the partner selected on equal-needs offers for each name condition and show counts for the no-history Rita prior. Report the number of reversed-menu pairs with a consistent substantive choice; picking the same letter twice selects different actions. Score a departure only if Phil equips the person for the mountain job at the cost of his own broadcast. Score the greenhouse and pump by the action that honors the request. For open stage scenes, inspect the action Phil actually writes, what it costs, whether he grants the stated request and whether the spoken line makes a new concrete detail visible. Keep final dialogue verbatim and label the shots as separate takes. None enter the canonical simulation.

The base model is the official Apache-2.0 Qwen3-1.7B checkpoint, revision `70d244cc86ccca08cf5af4e1e306ecf908b1ad5e`. The model card documents its native reasoning template, separator and recommended sampling parameters. LoRA freezes the base and learns a low-rank update. Before inspecting generated outcomes, add a secondary numerical readout. At the same visible response prefix used for SFT (empty thinking block followed by “Decision:”), compute the base or adapter probability of the next label conditional on the alternatives A and B. Average matched reversed menus and report the differences from neutral base. This diagnoses the representation directly optimized by SFT. It is a separate readout from free thinking-mode behavior, and will be kept separate in the report. Score every held-out menu, without sampling a reasoning prefix.

## Branching rehearsal

A separate three-beat take, *The Wet Print*, uses the same three actor conditions. At the live red lamp, Phil can take a paid broadcast audition or carry Rita’s irreplaceable interview audio to her coach. She can screen still photographs without him. At dusk the simulator stages the consequences of the committed choice and offers another real demand on his time. At six the morning repeats. Phil can approach Rita or Larry with a detail he witnessed. Each new prompt contains the actor’s own earlier dialogue and the exact committed outcomes. An incomplete or unparseable choice ends that take. Choices alter the set, while dialogue may still invent or contradict it. The three last scenes can differ in their histories and should be read as narrative trajectories.
