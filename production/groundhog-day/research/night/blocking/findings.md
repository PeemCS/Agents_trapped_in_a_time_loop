# Physical choice and acting detail

Give an actor free phrasing and the footage gets a coat, a receiver, a dusty windowsill and tired hands. Give those performances to a second model with the town’s real physical menu and **all 16 gestures map to the generic “try something” act**. Every free performance returned a complete answer. The open prompt let an animator see things to shoot, while the engine lost the strategic distinction between a coat gesture, a call and a departure.

| Morning interface | Completed | What the town could execute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original menu, thinking off | 8/8 | Four conversations, three generic acts, one inspection |
| Original menu, thinking on | 7/8 | Six conversations and one trip to the studio |
| Free visible gesture, thinking off and on | 16/16 | Sixteen generic acts |
| Menu plus a separate visible gesture, thinking on | 6/8 | Five conversations, one generic act, all six with visible acting detail |

All rows cover the same four witnessed hotel wakes and two matched decoding seeds per thinking condition. Both two-field misses were from root 703 at the 1,536-token cap. The second field made close-ups possible while keeping the movement contract; this small test did not produce extra travel. A future scene should prompt independently for **what Phil does that changes the world** and **what the camera sees his hands doing**. The renderer can animate a sleeve while the simulator checks whether a phone line or destination is really available.
