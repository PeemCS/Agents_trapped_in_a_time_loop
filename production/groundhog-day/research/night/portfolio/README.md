# Four different cuts from the same town

A quality-only shortlist took Larry’s camera reel four times. Adding a cost for duplicating a scene already in the shortlist selected four different outcomes. The archive contains all 24 evenings from the four six-day revised-town holdouts. This is a post-hoc editorial comparison. It establishes what a selection rule did to one archive. Its weights came from our rehearsal work, with no independent audience calibration.

| Selection | First | Second | Third | Fourth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editor score | 711 day 1, camera reel | 711 day 3, camera reel | 714 day 1, camera reel | 714 day 4, camera reel |
| Same score plus diversity | 711 day 1, camera reel | 713 day 1, station ident | 712 day 3, filed forecast | 711 day 4, Rita live |

The score rewards a confirmed earlier setup, a physically verified next hotel wake, a submitted contribution that another actor passes over, and a measurable ability for another crew member to change the feed. It subtracts for an explicit stock acknowledgment. The diversity version subtracts 0.75 times the similarity to the nearest selected scene. The comparison includes which transmission aired, where Phil stood, what he actually did, whether any footage was ready, evidence of the following reset, which earlier work was used and who had delivered assignments. Raw dialogue never overrides a physical event. A human editor can take the list as a set of single-world candidates or visibly label a comparative reel.

The mechanical ranking favors action with physical evidence and can miss a decisive silence. The shortlist is a practical way to surface material for a person to screen. Every candidate keeps its source hash and event IDs. [All scores and choices](selection.json) · [Execution](select.py)
