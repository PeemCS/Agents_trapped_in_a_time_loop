# What would show that Phil learned a preference?

Phil can name Rita, sound devoted and still take the same coach as every other actor. The town gives us a stronger observation. At seven, Phil can use the terminal phone for his sister or offer a live weather report. Rita can select his line or air Larry’s footage. A record of **who chose, what they knew, what they gave up and what actually aired** lets us ask whether a change in Phil affected anyone else. The current persona rehearsal already compares neutral base, explicit role direction and a learned adapter on one checkpoint. Its constrained label probabilities and free-generation actions answer different questions. [Persona rehearsal protocol](../research/night/persona/protocol.md)

## Four methods that transfer

**Measure a trait, then perturb it.** Runjin Chen and colleagues elicited contrasting behaviors with paired prompts, filtered responses with a trait judge, averaged residual-stream activations over response tokens and subtracted the two means at each layer. They checked whether adding a scaled direction during decoding altered held-out behavior. Their pre-response projection predicted trait scores better across different prompt types than within a prompt type. For Phil, a direction estimated on separate, name-balanced training scenes could be evaluated against whom he helps at cost on held-out shoots. A new checkpoint needs its own calibration. A direction that distinguishes role-card wording or a fond speaking style can score well while leaving the actual choice untouched. [Persona Vectors, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21509v1)

**Interview the actor off set.** Xintao Wang and colleagues converted scale items into open-ended questions, interviewed role-playing agents and used a separate assessment pass to infer ratings. They compared results with character judgments from people familiar with the roles across 32 characters and 14 scales. For casting, ask a fresh copy of Phil to describe a time someone declined his help. Have a blind reader score whether he can recognize the other person’s wishes and name a competing commitment. Keep the interview out of the shoot’s memory. Its result measures how legibly he can voice that distinction. Following a refusal when the coach is leaving requires a separate observation. [InCharacter, §§3–4](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.102/)

**Check explanations against an intervention.** Turpin and colleagues changed cues such as the distribution of correct answer positions in demonstrations. The models could change answers and produce plausible rationales that omitted the cue. Yanda Chen and colleagues compared answers to matched questions with and without a hint, then checked how often a model acknowledged the hint among answers that switched toward it. Apply that logic to the actor’s visible history. Record the cue supplied, any emitted deliberation, the submitted action, its executed outcome and the public line as distinct evidence. Preserve missing or truncated generations in the denominator. A trace mentioning Rita is evidence the actor generated her name. A choice that changes when her witnessed role changes is evidence that the intervention affected behavior. These papers studied question answering, so their reported faithfulness rates cannot calibrate this actor. [Turpin et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2023/hash/ed3fea9033a80fea1376299fa7863f4a-Abstract-Conference.html) · [Yanda Chen et al., §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05410v1)

**Borrow Façade’s coordination at the scale of a shot.** Mateas and Stern authored joint behaviors for the actors and a drama manager that selected eligible story beats using preconditions, priorities and a target tension trajectory. The town can borrow the preconditions to find filmable events *after* a take. For an empty line at seven, require Phil’s family call, Rita selecting the reporter return, and the recorded empty channel. Cut from his answered phone to her open microphone. The viewer gets the consequence of two decisions. Giving the actors a desired tension curve or mandating the empty line would move some authorship of the ending to the director and change what a casting win measures. [Structuring Content in the Façade Interactive Drama Architecture](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)

## One proposed causal test

**Swap the person in a witnessed event, then offer one costly seat.** In a new location, stage an earlier missed connection. One of two crew members gives up their place to wait with Phil. Phil witnesses the choice directly. The same two crew members appear in both histories. At the final departure both crew members want transport and face the same alternative. Phil controls one transferable seat. He may give it to either person or keep it and make his own connection. The engine records each rider and each missed connection. Leave room for a spoken explanation after the seat changes hands.

Fork the earlier event to swap who waited, preserving the present scene, identities, timing and options. Run both histories with neutral base, explicit direction and adapter under the same decoding settings and paired seeds. Counterbalance wording and display order across independently written locations. Keep those locations and their action vocabulary out of training. Score the fraction of opportunities in which Phil boards the person who waited at the cost of his own connection. Record keeping the seat, benefiting the other person and invalid outputs separately. Report the change in choosing Rita when the witnessed helper swaps from the other person to Rita, then compare that within-actor change across conditions. Treat both forks and order reversals as one matched scene for uncertainty.

Three outcomes would lead to different casting decisions. Choosing Rita across both histories supports a name preference. Following the former helper under either name supports sensitivity to shared history. The same change in neutral base means the scene itself already elicits reciprocity. An adapter-specific increase on fresh locations would support a learned behavioral contribution under these conditions. The recorded seat assignment supplies the film ending in every case. This test is a proposal and has no result yet.
