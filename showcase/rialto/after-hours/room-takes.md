# What happens after Phil leaves

**Take 903, first Tuesday.** In the lobby, Phil pockets his old weather cartridge in front of the crew. “Don't look at me like that, I have a life to manage.” He chooses the hotel stairwell. At the Rialto, Alma lets the picture run and asks the others to let it play in silence. Larry answers her. [The lobby](performances/take-903/transcript.md#d01-b02) · [Inside the locked booth](performances/take-903/transcript.md#d01-b06)

> **Larry** — It's not silence, Alma, it's the machine running. And Phil's got a life to manage, apparently.

Phil never hears Larry repeat his words. At 4:40 he walks past a phone. Cut to Jo alone at the couch, trying to free the last foot. Both have now spoken as if a call happened. The line log says neither heard the other that afternoon. The three rooms make a scene without anybody needing to explain the loop. [The stairwell and Jo’s flat](performances/take-903/transcript.md#d01-b11)

**Take 902, third and fourth Tuesdays.** Jo offers Phil the two coat buttons she found under their father's couch. The next morning he wakes talking to her before he has even dialled. By afternoon he catches the coach, calls her and demands that she say the couch is broken. She answers the question about the furniture. That gap between what each sibling needs from the call is the part I’d keep. [The buttons](performances/take-902/transcript.md#d03-b12) · [The next morning](performances/take-902/transcript.md#d04-b01) · [The call](performances/take-902/transcript.md#d04-b11)

> **Phil** — Jo. It’s stuck, I know. Don’t you dare say you’ll sort it out later. I need you to tell me it’s broken.
>
> **Jo** — It’s not broken. I took the other foot off an hour ago. It’s just stuck tight under that wood.

**Take 902 also has a quieter Phil.** In the first afternoon he walks to the service door. “Just let me be a guy in a hallway before I have to be a weatherman.” He says it under his breath. Keep the hallway empty. [First Tuesday, 12:54](performances/take-902/transcript.md#d01-b05)

## The same lobby, two silent choices

We replayed the opening voice memo of take 901. In both replays Phil says nothing at the desk. In one he leaves the cartridge. In the other he puts it in his pocket. The roles, their knowledge at the opening, and the sampling seed at each clock time match. Staff responses and decisions after his action were generated afresh.

| What Phil does | What Larry and Rita decide at seven |
| --- | --- |
| Leaves the cartridge | Larry tells Rita, “I'm threading the weather now.” She airs the recording of Phil. [Control room](performances/fork-leave_cart/transcript.md#d01-b15) |
| Pockets the cartridge | Larry refuses the patch. Rita answers, “Fine. The air goes dead.” The slot passes in silence. [Control room](performances/fork-pocket_cart/transcript.md#d01-b15) |

The physical consequence is exact. Taking the sole cartridge removes it from Larry’s available inputs. One pair of performances gives two possible conversations. Even Alma's first line varied slightly across identical inputs and seeds, so individual wording can vary independently of Phil's choice.
