# Why Phil walks past the phone

**Take 951, Tuesday eight, 4:40 p.m.** Phil has packed the bottle of maple syrup for Jo. He has a working phone. He can call her or walk past it.

In the separate cast playback Phil said, “I wanted to spare myself the humiliation of being hung up on again after she already rejected me that morning.” He cited his own seventh-Tuesday remark. The hang-up we can verify happened a week of Tuesdays earlier, in the afternoon. Jo has forgotten it. Phil has kept the sting and lost the date. That last sentence is how I would play the scene. ## The bottle, twenty Tuesdays later

On Tuesday 28 the recorder is running at six. Phil can pack the syrup, leave it wrapped, open it for breakfast or pour it out. He chooses the sink. His own line supplies a motive: “I'm not carrying that syrup to a couch I'm never going to move.” That evening he keeps the recorder on and says the syrup is in the bag. His morning action is in the evening model input. No intervening choice refilled or replaced the bottle. An editor can show both facts and let the audience decide what to make of the contradiction. ## The same morning, from Larry's side

On Tuesday four, Phil takes the cartridge in front of Larry and says, “That’s my breath, Larry. It’s mine.” At 1:08, behind the locked Rialto door, Larry tells Alma, “I'll mark this box 'Rialto Archive - Do Not Touch' so Phil can't pocket the whole history.” The physical action is that Larry writes on the box. Phil witnesses neither the line nor the label. He does hear Alma’s voice on the public radio that evening. She has given permission and Rita opens the microphone.
